An Admin connects messaging services at platform level. Each user then binds their own identity and chooses notification subscriptions in their account.

## Choose a channel

| Channel | Platform credentials | Personal identity |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Email](/docs/guides/automation/im/email/) | SMTP; optional IMAP for conversations | Account email |
| [Slack](/docs/guides/automation/im/slack/) | Bot and app tokens | Member ID |
| [Telegram](/docs/guides/automation/im/telegram/) | Bot token | Numeric user ID |
| [Discord](/docs/guides/automation/im/discord/) | Bot token | User ID |
| [Lark](/docs/guides/automation/im/lark/) | App ID and App Secret | App-scoped open_id |
| [Feishu](/docs/guides/automation/im/feishu/) | App ID and App Secret | App-scoped open_id |

Display names, group IDs, and bot IDs are not interchangeable with personal user IDs.

## Connect and verify

Follow the channel's creation, permission, installation, and event-reception steps. Save platform credentials, enter your own identity in your account, and enable desired notifications.

For AI conversations, also configure a [model](/docs/guides/automation/models/). Start with a read-only question and confirm the account and deployment. Ordinary notifications do not require AI.

Entering another person's ID can expose your notifications and account permissions to that identity. Verify your own immutable ID before saving.

## Troubleshoot by layer

Check the platform app and credentials, network connection, event permissions, personal binding, subscription, and model separately. A working avatar lookup does not prove message delivery or correct account binding.

Use [notification troubleshooting](/docs/guides/automation/im/notifications/) for role filtering, language, and delivery failures.
